*GMI13: equitable data sharing with William Hsiao*

Andrew Page and Lee Katz speak with Dr William Hsiao on day two of the Global Microbial Identifier conference, GMI13, in Vancouver, Canada. Hsiao explains how he and Emma Griffiths approached organising a meeting with equity, diversity and interoperability in mind. They discuss participation from low- and middle-income countries, First Nations representation, and why social perspectives matter when designing technical approaches to data sharing.

## In this episode

### Four years in the making

William Hsiao introduces himself as working at Simon Fraser University and describes organising GMI13 with Emma Griffiths. They agreed to host in 2019, before the pandemic, making the event four years in the making. Hsiao distinguishes that long wait from the few months spent organising it. Both hosts say they are enjoying the meeting, with Lee praising its organisation.

### Building more equitable data sharing

Hsiao describes a growing emphasis within GMI on equity and diversity, including greater participation from low- and middle-income countries. He points to the previous meeting in Singapore as an effort to support travel logistics for communities in Asia.

For Vancouver, the organisers wanted to build on that experience through social sciences and socially oriented discussions. The aim was to improve data sharing and make it more equitable, rather than assume one solution would fit everyone.

### Representation and the work behind invitations

Andrew welcomes the inclusion of people from First Nations and marginalised communities. Hsiao explains that a major First Nations conference was taking place across town, making invitations difficult because several groups were already attending that event. They eventually found someone relevant who could join, and he credits Emma Griffiths with the practical work of resolving invitations.

Andrew also praises the meeting's gender balance and notes that there have been no male-only panels so far.

### Connecting social perspectives to interoperability

Hsiao says participant engagement has exceeded expectations. The discussions have also been more diverse and richer than the organisers' initial ideas about where conversations would go.

When Lee brings the conversation back to bioinformatics, Hsiao identifies interoperability as another focus of the conference. He argues that considering social perspectives while designing technological solutions can improve buy-in and make practice more equitable. The discussion stays at this level rather than examining particular software or implementation methods.

### A Vancouver coffee and doughnut verdict

Lee asks for Hsiao's opinion on his arrival-night purchase: a Boston cream doughnut from Tim Hortons. Hsiao says Vancouver has better doughnut shops, but recommends ordering a double double coffee as a Canadian gesture. The exchange explains that this means two sugars and two creams, not a double espresso.
